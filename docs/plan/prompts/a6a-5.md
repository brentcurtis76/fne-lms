**r4 lost four shapes and disclosed one.** The old `[.,\s]?` separator matched
every Unicode space `\s` covers; the new `NUMBER_TOKEN` grammar enumerates a
narrower set. That is the defect, and it is the whole blocking scope of this
round.

Note also: **your newline rationale was wrong.** `€70\n120` fires on the r3
scanner too, so excluding newline bought nothing — there was no band-fusion to
prevent. Restore newline along with the rest.

## 1 — Restore the lost coverage (blocking)

Make the separator class cover at least everything `\s` did, plus NBSP and the
narrow/thin spaces: U+0009, U+000A, U+000D, U+0020, U+00A0, U+2009, U+202F, and
the general Unicode space separators. Keep the normalisation architecture from
r4 — it is the right design and the r1 S1 false-positive controls it introduced
must all stay green. This is a widening of one character class, not a rewrite.

Also restore `€2.500e3`: r4's exponent handling consumes a grouped mantissa
incorrectly. Handle a scientific mantissa that itself contains a grouping
separator, and shift the decimal in both directions.

## 2 — Make this class of regression impossible to ship again (blocking)

The real failure here is not the four shapes — it is that a rewrite silently
dropped coverage and the report disclosed one case out of four, because only the
new cases were tested and the old ones were assumed.

Add a **differential corpus test**: a single list of representative amount
spellings, asserted case by case with its expected verdict. Any future change
that stops firing on a shape in that list fails, without anyone having to
remember to re-test it. Seed it with every row in the table above, each with the
verdict it should have **after** this round. Where a row stays a known miss,
pin it as an explicit expected-miss with a comment saying why — so the limit is
recorded rather than rediscovered.

## 3 — Close the remaining tail by policy, not by chasing it (blocking, cheap)

Do **not** implement fullwidth-digit or other-script digit normalisation, and do
not fix the two pre-existing false alarms (`€2500e-1`, `€2.500e0`). Both are
firing conservatively, which is the safe direction for a guard, and neither
arises from any realistic path into this bundle.

Instead, write the threat model into the file as a comment and pin the limits in
the corpus: the realistic leak vectors are (a) the commercial module reaching a
client bundle, where the minifier emits the *shortest* numeric form — plain
digits or `2.5e3`, never `2.500e3` or `25000e-1` — and (b) a human typing or
pasting a price into JSX, which is where NBSP and narrow/thin spaces come from
(design tools emit them) and where fullwidth digits do not. State that the guard
covers those two vectors and deliberately does not attempt arbitrary Unicode
digit normalisation.

If you disagree with that scoping after looking, say so as a finding — do not
silently widen it.
