## 1. THE BUG

`get_bucket_summary` (`supabase/migrations/00000000000000_baseline.sql:2781-2820`) builds
`effective_allocations` (direct + annex), then:

```sql
FROM effective_allocations ea
JOIN hour_types ht ON ht.id = ea.hour_type_id
LEFT JOIN contract_hours_ledger chl ON chl.allocation_id = ea.id
  AND chl.status IN ('reservada', 'consumida', 'penalizada')
GROUP BY ht.key, ht.display_name, ht.sort_order
```

and aggregates `SUM(ea.allocated_hours)` over that joined set.

**The `LEFT JOIN` fans out.** One allocation with N matching ledger rows becomes N rows, so
`SUM(ea.allocated_hours)` counts that allocation's hours **N times**. `allocated_hours`,
`available_hours` and `annex_hours` are all inflated by the ledger's row count.

A 100-hour allocation with three sessions on it reports **300 allocated** and
`300 − reserved − consumed` available. The error grows every time a session is booked, and
it always errs toward **telling a school it has more hours than it bought.**

`reserved_hours` and `consumed_hours` are correct — they sum `chl.hours`, and the fan-out
gives each ledger row exactly once.

**A second copy of the same formula exists** at
`supabase/migrations/20260805120000_reschedule_hours_rpc.sql:163-171`, which computes
availability inline and documents itself as matching `get_bucket_summary`. It must end up
consistent with the repaired function — Sol asked for **one formula**.

## 2. PM RULINGS

**Ruling 1 — aggregate allocations and ledger separately, then combine.** Sum allocations
once per hour type in their own aggregate; sum ledger consumption once per hour type in
another; join the two results. Do not attempt to patch the fan-out with `DISTINCT` on a
sum — an allocation's hours are not distinct from another allocation's equal hours, and
`SUM(DISTINCT …)` would silently swallow two legitimately-equal allocations.

**Ruling 2 — `CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION` at the identical signature. NO `DROP`.** This is
exactly the rule r21 was dispatched to repair, and repeating it here would be worse than the
original. The signature is
`public.get_bucket_summary(p_contrato_id uuid) RETURNS TABLE(hour_type_key text,
display_name text, allocated_hours numeric, reserved_hours numeric, consumed_hours numeric,
available_hours numeric, is_fixed_allocation boolean, annex_hours numeric)`.
**The return shape must not change** — column names, order and types all stay, because
`lib/types/hour-tracking.types.ts:131` and every dashboard consumer read it positionally by
name. If you believe the shape must change, **stop and report `FINDINGS`.**

**Ruling 3 — grants stay exactly as they are.** The baseline grants EXECUTE to `anon`,
`authenticated` and `service_role` (`baseline.sql:23723-23725`). `CREATE OR REPLACE`
preserves them; **do not add a blanket REVOKE**, and do not "tighten while you're in there".
Assert the three grants survive.

**Ruling 4 — one formula, and the reschedule RPC adopts it.** After the repair, the inline
expression at `20260805120000:163-171` must agree with `get_bucket_summary` for every case
your tests cover. Prefer having the RPC **call** `get_bucket_summary` over keeping a second
hand-maintained copy; if calling it is not workable, say why in the report and prove
agreement by test instead.

**Ruling 5 — this changes numbers real users currently see.** Dashboards will show *fewer*
